## #1 Fusion
- Website: https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/personal
- Pricing: Freemium
- Use it for: CAD and CAM in one, from design to toolpaths
- In plain English: Design the part and make the toolpaths in the same program. Free for personal, non-commercial use (under $1,000 a year), with limits like 10 active designs and 3-axis CAM. Its AI Assistant is still a preview, and generative design needs a paid plan.

## #2 Onshape
- Website: https://www.onshape.com
- Pricing: Freemium
- Use it for: Browser-based CAD that runs on any computer
- In plain English: Full 3D CAD in your web browser, no install. Free plan, but your designs are public. Includes an AI Advisor that answers how-to questions.

## #3 Zoo Zookeeper
- Website: https://zoo.dev/zookeeper
- Pricing: Freemium
- Use it for: Describe a part in words and get an editable 3D CAD model
- In plain English: Type something like "a flange with 4 bolt holes" and it makes an editable CAD model you can export as STEP. Works best on simple parts. Free plan includes $10 of AI use a month.

## #4 Tinkercad
- Website: https://www.tinkercad.com
- Pricing: Free
- Use it for: Very simple 3D designs for printing
- In plain English: Autodesk's free beginner tool. Not AI, but the easiest way to make a 3D-printable part in an afternoon.

## #5 ChatGPT
- Website: https://chatgpt.com
- Pricing: Freemium
- Use it for: Explain and draft G-code, feeds and speeds
- In plain English: Good for learning what G-code lines do or drafting a simple program from your dimensions and tool. It can get things wrong, so always simulate and dry-run above the part before cutting.
